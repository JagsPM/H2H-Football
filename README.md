What is it?

A free tool for comparing two football teams side by side, across leagues and countries. Put Arsenal up against Bayern Munich, or Benfica against Juventus. The site is available in Danish, English, Spanish and German (pick the language at the top). No account, app or installation is needed. Just open the link.

It covers the Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1, Eredivisie and Primeira Liga, plus around 70 other clubs that have played in the Champions League, Europa League or Conference League in recent years.

How to use it
Pick teams. Choose a league and then a team on both sides. Swap switches team A and B.
Choose a view.
Elo-adjusted takes opponent strength into account. Use it when comparing teams from different leagues.
Raw data shows simple figures (points, goals, clean sheets) with no adjustment. They say nothing about how strong or weak the opponents were.
Choose a period. Look at the last 5, 10, 20 or 40 matches, and choose league matches only or all matches.
Read the model prediction. The most likely outcome, the most likely score, expected goals and the probabilities of a home win, draw and away win.
Choose the venue. Team A at home, team B at home or neutral ground. Home advantage matters a lot.
Compare with your odds. Under Fair odds and value you see the model's probability and the "fair" odds (1 divided by the probability). Type the odds you have been offered into Your odds and the Edge is shown. A positive edge means the model thinks the odds are higher than the probability justifies. If you enter all three 1X2 odds, the bookmaker's margin is shown too.
See the trend. The chart shows both teams' Elo since 2019, and below it you find recent form and previous meetings.
What is Elo?

Every team has one number for its strength. If a team beats a strong opponent, the number rises a lot. If it loses to a weak one, it falls a lot. Big wins move the number more than narrow ones. The leagues are linked through the European competitions, so the numbers can be compared across countries. On average the Premier League teams rank highest, followed by La Liga and the Bundesliga.

How good is the model?

I tested the model on matches it was not fitted on (all league and European matches after July 2024, over 5,000 matches):

It points to the right outcome (home win, draw or away win) in about 53% of matches. Always picking a home win would be right about 44% of the time.
Its probabilities fit reality about 9% better than a simple guess based on how often each outcome generally happens.

That is useful, but it is not a win rate for betting. Bookmakers know more than the model does, and the test was done without real odds. Use the site to check whether odds look off, not to bet blindly.

What the model does not know

Injuries, suspensions, squads, managers' choices, motivation, weather and how many chances the teams create. It only knows results. Teams outside the seven leagues are rated only on their European matches, so their numbers are less certain. The Europa League and Conference League data stops in May 2025 in the dataset, so teams that only play there may be out of date.

Data and updates

Match data comes from the openfootball project (public domain). The site is rebuilt automatically every Monday and picks up the latest matches openfootball has published. If the source is behind, so is the site. The date of the latest league match is shown at the top of the page.
